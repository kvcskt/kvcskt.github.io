Zürich,
Stadt der Brunnen

the context

the client

Zürich, Stadt der Brunnen was a CAS UX/UI project at ZHdK. The brief was to create a website that showcases open data from opendata.swiss. We used the City of Zurich’s art collection dataset to build an interactive map of the city’s artistic and historical fountains. Visitors can put together their own path from fountain to fountain and discover each one’s history, architecture and artistic details.

the goal

We wanted to highlight Zurich’s fountains and let art lovers from around the world experience them as an open-air gallery. We planned three features:


• Interactive map: it shows the fountains from the city’s art collection.
• Detail view: clicking a fountain opens its description in a modal window.
• Fountain path (“Brunnenpfad”): users build their own path, the map guides them from fountain to fountain, and the current next fountain always appears in the detail view.

my role

I worked in a team of three with Angelo Gramatica and Shirin Debirzadeh over four months: concept, wireframes, UI design and prototyping.

the approach

target audience

Art-interested people of all ages: Zurich residents, Swiss tourists, international visitors, school classes and culture lovers.

the problem

This project didn’t start from a user problem. We wanted to create something that highlights Zurich’s fountains, and open data was the starting material. The challenge was turning a dataset into an experience people want to explore. The art collection’s website didn’t offer the background information on each fountain that makes a walk worthwhile, so we had to build that content ourselves. We also had to decide how a visitor could pick a handful of fountains from a very large number and discover them in a sensible order.

the process

We started with a wireframe, and each of us designed a homepage from it. We explored a lot, but the designs drifted further from the wireframe, and it became hard to settle on a visual direction. In a team of three with different opinions, we couldn’t decide. We also loved some designs that didn’t fit the chosen direction, and letting them go was difficult. So we took two steps back and began again with new wireframes that defined the basic functionality. Angelo and I built the homepage on top of them, and Shirin created the Brunnenwissen page.

Because the art collection’s site lacked fountain details, I gathered interesting information on each fountain myself. The Brunnenwissen content was written from a Brunnenguide brochure, and we pre-edited the images in Photoshop. For the brand, we wanted to keep the elegant look of Zurich’s own brand but add a fresh twist with pink and red highlight colours. The simple typeface balances the detailed, very diverse look of the fountains.



the outcome

The prototype is a two-view homepage. The first view is a grid of Zurich’s historical fountains, where users can click a fountain for more information and add it to their own path. The second view is a map showing the selected fountains linked by a path, ready to be discovered. Users can add or remove fountains there too, and share the path via a link. Shirin’s Brunnenwissen page adds background facts about the fountains, such as how their water is supplied.

We ran out of time to test the prototype with users, so it remains an untested proposal.

What we took away:

• Teamwork: a team of three brings many ideas and needs clear project leadership.
• Focus: take the bird’s-eye view, reduce, and kill your darlings.
• Foundation: a strong foundation is essential, which is why starting over from the wireframe paid off.
